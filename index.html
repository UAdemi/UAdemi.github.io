Fragmented information, then the relationships between it, then one path from question to outcome. That sequence is the job, whether the setting is research, data, or a business.

One way of working, across different settings

I am completing a Ph.D. in Political Science at Northwestern University and hold an M.S. in Applied Statistics. My work has taken me from developing new methods for causal inference to building large public datasets, growing a consumer business, and communicating complex ideas to broad audiences.

The settings differ, but the underlying challenge is usually the same: the problem is important, the information is incomplete, and there is no ready-made playbook.

Selected work

Understanding interventions that change the systems around them

Most causal methods assume that the relationships through which effects spread remain fixed. My dissertation asks what happens when an intervention changes the network itself — and develops methods for evaluating the resulting trajectory.

Research · Causal inference · Network analysis — Explore the dissertation

Turning fragmented records into usable evidence

For a $49,500 National Science Foundation project, I transformed dispersed public records into a reusable dataset of more than 300,000 observations spanning over a decade. The work required building the data infrastructure, resolving inconsistencies, and converting raw information into a foundation for analysis.

Building and growing a consumer business

I helped launch and grow a coffee business that generated approximately $180,000 in revenue during its first 15 months. My work included growth strategy, digital marketing, performance analysis, and the repeated operational decisions required to build with limited resources.

Communicating complex ideas at scale

Across university teaching, public analysis, and digital media, I have worked to make technical and political ideas accessible without making them simplistic. This has included teaching statistics to more than 150 students and contributing to a social-science media platform serving a community of more than 380,000 subscribers.

Current focus

How should we evaluate a decision when it changes not only outcomes, but also the system through which those outcomes unfold?

My current research develops causal methods for settings in which policies reshape evolving networks. The broader aim is to make credible evaluation possible when interventions, relationships, and behaviour change together over time.

Read about my research

The common thread

Whether I am studying a changing political network, building a dataset, evaluating a marketing decision, or explaining a technical result, my approach is consistent:

  • Define the real question.
  • Structure the available evidence.
  • Make the decision.
  • Learn from what happens next.
