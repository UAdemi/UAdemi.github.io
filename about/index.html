About

The common thread

My work has taken me from archival records on late Ottoman nationalist movements to large public datasets, causal inference for evolving networks, digital media, and building a coffee business. The settings differ, but the underlying question is consistent: how do people make consequential decisions inside systems that keep changing around them?

What I work on now

I am completing a Ph.D. in Political Science at Northwestern University, where I also hold an M.S. in Applied Statistics. My dissertation develops causal methods for settings in which an intervention changes not only outcomes, but also the network through which those outcomes unfold.

Alongside the dissertation I build research data infrastructure, most recently a public-records dataset of more than 300,000 observations for a National Science Foundation project, and I teach empirical and statistical research methods.

Why the range holds together

Research taught me to define a question precisely enough that evidence can answer it. Statistics taught me to be honest about uncertainty. Entrepreneurship taught me something neither of those does: that at some point the analysis stops and you have to decide, with the information you actually have, and then own what follows.

Teaching and public writing taught me the last part — that an argument no one can follow is not yet finished.

Background

I studied Political Science and International Relations at Boğaziçi University in Istanbul, completing a B.A. with a double major in History and Dean’s High Honors, followed by an M.A. My graduate work there focused on elite networks in nationalist movements — which is where the dataset behind much of my later research began.

I am also a graduate of the Abdullah Tivnikli ISAR Foundation four-year program, which combines the classical study of Madrasah education with a rigorous Arabic language curriculum, and I studied Modern Standard Arabic at the Qasid Institute in Amman.

I am a native speaker of Turkish and Albanian, work professionally in English and Arabic, and read French and Ottoman Turkish. That range is not incidental to the research: the elite-network dataset could not have been built without it.
