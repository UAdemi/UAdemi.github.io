Causal inference for evolving networks

Problem
Standard causal methods assume the relationships through which an effect travels stay fixed. In the settings I study, they do not — the intervention itself reshapes the network, and the usual estimators stop meaning what they claim to mean.
What I did
My dissertation develops methods for evaluating an intervention when the structure through which its effects spread is itself changing, and applies them to political networks observed over time.
Result
An approach to estimating effects along a trajectory rather than at a single point — ongoing doctoral research at Northwestern.
What it demonstrates
Working at the point where an existing method breaks down, and building something defensible rather than applying a familiar tool to a problem it does not fit.

Building with data

Problem
A $49,500 National Science Foundation project depended on evidence that did not yet exist in usable form. The underlying records were public, but dispersed across sources, formats, and more than a decade of inconsistent reporting.
What I did
Built the data infrastructure end to end: collection, reconciliation of inconsistent records, validation, and the transformation of raw public filings into a structured analytical foundation.
Result
A reusable dataset of more than 300,000 observations spanning over a decade, now serving as the empirical base for ongoing analysis.
What it demonstrates
Comfort with messy, incomplete, real-world information — and the discipline to build infrastructure other people can rely on, rather than a one-off analysis.

Building a business

Problem
Launching a consumer coffee business with limited capital, no established brand, and no margin for slow decisions.
What I did
Growth strategy, digital marketing, pricing and product decisions, and performance analysis — then the repeated operational calls required to keep a young business moving under real constraints.
Result
Approximately $180,000 in revenue over the first 15 months.
What it demonstrates
Ownership of outcomes rather than recommendations. Acting under uncertainty with incomplete information, and living with the consequences of the decision.

Communicating at scale

Problem
Technical and political ideas lose their substance when they are simplified for general audiences, and lose their audience when they are not.
What I did
Contributed to 49W, a social-science media platform, alongside published analysis in English and Turkish for outlets including Anadolu Agency and Mesail — media production and public writing aimed at holding both rigour and reach.
Result
Work reaching a community of more than 380,000 subscribers.
What it demonstrates
Translating complexity for non-specialist audiences — the same skill any client presentation demands.

Teaching and leadership

Problem
Statistics is where many social science students decide they are not quantitative people. Reversing that means meeting very different levels of technical background in the same room.
What I did
Taught empirical and statistical research methods at Northwestern, supervised research teams, and coordinated the Comparative Politics Workshop across an academic year.
Result
More than 150 students taught, with a 5.4 out of 6 teaching evaluation.
What it demonstrates
Structuring an explanation for the audience in front of you, and leading people rather than only analysis.
